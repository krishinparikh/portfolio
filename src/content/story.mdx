I was twelve when my middle school Quiz Bowl coach floated the idea of purchasing a buzzer system for our practices. You know those game shows where contestants race to hit a button first? That's basically what we needed. My teammates started searching online for viable options, but my instinct was a little different. Instead, I thought to myself: *what if I could build it?*

And so, I did. After weeks of YouTube tutorials and Radio Shack visits, I had a working buzzer system. Was it pretty janky in hindsight? Absolutely. But most importantly, that was among my first experiences that revealed the pattern that has defined my life since:

*Recognize a problem, become obsessively curious, and build a solution.*

That pattern continued the following year, when I turned a Rubik's Cube obsession into a summer camp, teaching kids to solve it — a skill I had honed to [under 15 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2yrVTqqy4yc). I also launched an e-commerce site selling [custom hip-hop beats](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-atg20iRoEA) I'd produced in my bedroom.

High school taught me that building great products requires building great teams — and setting the right culture — to coalesce around a shared vision. As Senior Class President, I spearheaded multiple initiatives to help reconnect our class following the disruptions of COVID and broader social challenges. Leading the Yearbook and Mock Trial sharpened my ability to craft and tell compelling stories — a skill that has equipped me well for the world of business.

Entering college, I naturally wanted to learn about both technology and business — not just one or the other — so I chose to double major in Computer Science and Finance. In my freshman year, I applied an [EdTech startup idea](/projects/peer-ed) of mine to [Venture @ CWRU](https://linkedin.com/company/neosvf-cwru/), a student-run venture fund. While the startup didn't work out, I inadvertently got dragged into the fund as an analyst/investor, teaching me about the world of venture capital and how to build a much better business in the future. Under my leadership, Venture @ CWRU grew from just 4 to 25+ recurring members, becoming one of the most active business organizations on campus.

I've further pursued venture capital as President of the [Alumni Venture Fund](https://case.edu/entrepreneurship/alumni-venture-fund), where I've gotten the opportunity to source deals and make investments of up to $25,000 per company. I was also 1 of 5 members on Case Western's [Venture Capital Investment Competition](http://vcic.org) (VCIC) team, where we placed 3rd in our region for our outstanding due diligence and deal memo construction.

To better understand the ins-and-outs of startups, I interned at [ImmersiveTouch](http://immersivetouch.com) during the summer of 2023, where I researched competitors and learned about healthcare sales cycles and processes. That fall, I was 1 of 12 selected [Veale Snyder Fellows](https://case.edu/entrepreneurship/vealesnyderfellows), allowing me to take fully funded trips to Silicon Valley, Las Vegas (for the Consumer Electronics Show), and Ireland to visit tech companies and network with professionals.

That Fellowship opened many doors, including my internship at [Afference](http://afference.io), a startup whose mission is to bring the sense of touch — a key aspect of human connection — to the digital world. I helped build and design a software development kit that will be deployed alongside Afference products, allowing software developers to integrate complex haptics into their digital experiences.

Most recently, I led the development of an internal CRM platform for The O.H.I.O. Fund, from inception to launch. It reinforced my desire to not just write code behind a screen, but oversee the entire lifecycle of a product — including user research, design, coding, and continuous iteration. Using this experience, I also built a CRM for Case's Alumni Venture Fund, gaining significant expertise in this niche of AI x VC.

These days, I'm captivated by the opportunities of AI, especially in spaces that traditionally lack technical innovation. Drawing from my technical expertise and business/startup experience, I enjoy searching for and building high-value AI applications. Some projects include a [personalized education app](/projects/curio), a [digital mind chatbot](/projects/consultant-digital-mind), and automations for podcast episode analysis and personalized networking.

My most recent projet is [Lineage](/projects/lineage), a platform I'm building with my co-founder [Kaleb Kim](https://www.linkedin.com/in/kaleb-k/) that helps older adults turn their life stories into narrated, cinematic videos. It was inspired by the passing of my grandmother and the realization of how many of her stories we never captured. Lineage combines AI-guided storytelling conversations, collaborative script editing, and automated video generation to make family story preservation as easy as having a conversation. We've raised over $6,000 in non-dilutive funding and are preparing to launch our beta soon.
